# Module: Understanding AI
What AI is, what it isn't, and how machines "learn".

## Lesson: What is artificial intelligence? (8 min)
**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is the field of building computer systems that can do tasks that normally need human intelligence — understanding language, recognising images, making decisions, or creating new text and pictures.

### AI you already use
- **Phone keyboards** that predict your next word.
- **Face unlock** on your phone.
- **Google Maps** predicting traffic and the fastest route.
- **YouTube and TikTok** recommending videos you might like.
- **Spam filters** keeping junk out of your email.
- **Translation** apps converting English to Twi, Ewe, Hausa or French.
- **Fraud detection** spotting unusual mobile money or bank transactions.
- **AI assistants** such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot that answer questions and write text.

### Narrow AI vs general AI
- **Narrow AI** is designed for one kind of task — recognising faces, recommending videos, translating text. All AI today is narrow, even when it seems very clever.
- **Artificial general intelligence (AGI)** would match human ability across almost any task. It does not exist yet, and experts disagree about when, or whether, it will.

### What AI is not
- AI is **not magic** — it's mathematics and data running on computers.
- AI does **not "understand"** the world the way people do, and it can be confidently wrong.
- AI has **no feelings, intentions or opinions** of its own, even if it sounds friendly.

> [!TIP]
> A helpful way to think about AI assistants: a very well-read helper who works fast, makes mistakes sometimes, and needs clear instructions and careful checking.

## Lesson: How machines learn (9 min)
Traditional programs follow rules written by people: *"If the score is 50 or more, print Pass."* **Machine learning (ML)** is different: instead of writing all the rules, we show the computer many **examples**, and it finds the patterns itself.

### Learning from examples
Imagine teaching a computer to recognise ripe and unripe mangoes:
1. Collect thousands of mango photos, each **labelled** "ripe" or "unripe". This is the **training data**.
2. The algorithm studies the photos and adjusts itself to find patterns (colour, texture, shape).
3. Test it on **new photos** it has never seen. If it's accurate, it has learned.
4. Use it — for example, in an app that helps farmers sort mangoes.

### Three types of machine learning
| Type | How it learns | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Supervised learning** | From labelled examples | Is this email spam or not? Will this loan be repaid? |
| **Unsupervised learning** | Finds groups and patterns in unlabelled data | Grouping customers with similar shopping habits |
| **Reinforcement learning** | Learns by trial and error with rewards | AI learning to play games; robots learning to walk |

### Neural networks and deep learning
Many modern AI systems use **neural networks** — layers of connected mathematical "neurons" loosely inspired by the brain. With many layers and huge amounts of data this is called **deep learning**, and it powers image recognition, speech recognition and AI chatbots.

### Data is everything
An AI system is only as good as its data.
- **Too little data** → it doesn't learn well.
- **Poor-quality data** → it learns mistakes.
- **Biased data** → it learns unfair patterns. (If a face-recognition system is trained mostly on lighter-skinned faces, it may work worse for darker-skinned people — a real problem that has been documented.)

## Lesson: Generative AI and large language models (9 min)
**Generative AI** creates new content: text, images, music, video and code.

### Large language models (LLMs)
AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are powered by **large language models**. They were trained on enormous amounts of text to predict **what words are likely to come next**. By doing this extremely well, they can answer questions, summarise, translate, write and explain.

Because they generate likely-sounding text rather than looking facts up, they can sometimes produce **confident but false information**. This is called a **hallucination**. Some assistants can search the web or read documents you provide, which helps — but you should still check important facts.

### Other generative AI
- **Image generators** create pictures from text descriptions.
- **Voice AI** can read text aloud or copy a person's voice.
- **Video AI** can create or edit video.
- **Code assistants** help programmers write and debug code.

### Strengths and weaknesses of AI assistants
| Good at | Weak at |
|---|---|
| Explaining concepts in different ways | Guaranteeing facts are correct |
| Drafting and editing text | Very recent events (unless connected to search) |
| Brainstorming ideas | Personal or local knowledge it was never given |
| Summarising long documents | Complex maths without careful checking |
| Translating and simplifying language | Knowing when it's wrong |

> [!WARNING]
> Never paste passwords, private personal information, or confidential work or school documents into public AI tools unless you are sure how your data will be used.

> Ask an AI assistant a question you already know the answer to — for example about your town or school. Did it get it right? Did it make anything up?

# Module: Using AI tools well
Get great results from AI assistants for study and work.

## Lesson: Prompting — how to ask AI good questions (10 min)
A **prompt** is the instruction you give an AI. Better prompts get much better results. A useful formula is **R-T-C-F**:

- **Role** — who should the AI act as? *"You are a patient maths tutor for JHS students."*
- **Task** — what exactly should it do? *"Explain how to solve simultaneous equations."*
- **Context** — what background does it need? *"I understand single equations but get confused when there are two unknowns."*
- **Format** — how should the answer look? *"Use one worked example with steps numbered, then give me two practice questions without answers."*

### Weak vs strong prompts
❌ *"Write about climate change."*

✅ *"You are a science teacher. Write a 200-word explanation of how climate change affects farming in northern Ghana, for SHS 1 students. Use simple language, include two local examples, and end with one discussion question."*

### Prompting tips
- **Be specific** about length, audience, tone and format (bullet points, table, email).
- **Give examples** of what you want: *"Write it in a style like this: …"*
- **Ask for steps**: *"Think step by step"* or *"Show your working"* helps with maths and logic.
- **Iterate**: treat it as a conversation. *"Make it shorter."* *"Use simpler words."* *"Add a Ghanaian example."*
- **Ask it to ask you**: *"Before you answer, ask me any questions you need."*
- **Request sources** — then check that they actually exist and say what the AI claims.

### Useful prompt templates
- *"Explain [topic] to me like I'm 12, then like I'm a university student."*
- *"Here is my answer to [question]. Point out mistakes and explain how to improve it, but don't rewrite it for me."*
- *"Create a 5-question multiple-choice quiz on [topic] with answers at the end."*
- *"Turn these notes into a clear summary with headings: [paste notes]."*

> [!TRY]
> Write a weak prompt and a strong R-T-C-F prompt for the same request. Try both with an AI assistant and compare the answers.

## Lesson: AI for learning — your study partner (9 min)
Used well, AI can be an excellent personal tutor, available any time. Used badly, it can stop you from learning at all.

### Great ways to learn with AI
- **Explain differently:** "I don't understand photosynthesis. Explain it using an example from a farm."
- **Quiz yourself:** "Ask me 10 questions about the causes of World War I, one at a time, and tell me if I'm right."
- **Check your understanding:** "Here's my explanation of supply and demand. What did I get wrong?"
- **Practise languages:** have conversations in French and ask for corrections.
- **Break down big tasks:** "Help me make a two-week revision plan for my exams."
- **Coding help:** "Why does my Python loop never stop?" (paste your code).

### The golden rule: AI should help you think, not think for you
If AI does your homework, you get the marks but not the knowledge — and exams, interviews and real jobs will reveal the gap. Use AI to **understand**, then do the work yourself.

### Academic honesty
- Follow your school's or employer's rules about AI.
- Submitting AI-written work as your own, when it isn't allowed, is **plagiarism** — even if no one notices.
- If you used AI, say so when required: *"I used an AI assistant to help brainstorm ideas and check grammar."*
- Teachers increasingly ask students to explain their work aloud — make sure you can.

### Always verify
AI can be wrong about facts, dates, formulas and references. Check important information in your textbook, trusted websites or with your teacher.

> [!TIP]
> Ask the AI to act as a "Socratic tutor": *"Don't give me the answer. Ask me guiding questions until I solve it myself."* It's one of the most powerful ways to learn.

> [!TRY]
> Pick a topic you find difficult. Ask an AI to tutor you Socratically for 10 minutes, then write a summary in your own words.

## Lesson: AI for work and creativity (9 min)
AI tools can make many jobs faster — for writers, marketers, small business owners, programmers, teachers and designers.

### Practical uses at work
- **Writing:** draft emails, reports, social media captions, CVs and cover letters — then edit them in your own voice.
- **Summarising:** turn long documents or meeting notes into key points and action items.
- **Data:** explain spreadsheet formulas, suggest charts, help analyse survey results.
- **Customer service:** draft replies to common questions.
- **Marketing:** brainstorm campaign ideas and product names.
- **Coding:** generate, explain and debug code.
- **Design:** generate image ideas, mood boards and drafts in tools like Canva's AI features.
- **Translation:** draft translations for wider audiences (and have a fluent speaker check them).

### Example: a small business owner
Abena runs a juice business in Koforidua. She uses AI to:
1. brainstorm ten names for a new pineapple-ginger flavour,
2. write five WhatsApp status captions for the launch,
3. create a simple price list table,
4. draft a polite reply to a customer complaint.

She then edits everything so it sounds like *her*, checks prices and facts, and adds her own photos.

### Keep a human in the loop
- **Review everything** before sending or publishing.
- **Add your own knowledge**, experience and local context — that's what makes work valuable.
- **Protect confidential information** — don't paste customer data or private business documents into public AI tools.
- **Be transparent** where it matters (for example, AI-generated images in news or advertising).

> Use an AI assistant to draft a cover letter for a job or internship you'd like. Then rewrite it so it truly reflects your experience and voice.

# Module: AI, society and the future
Think critically about the risks, ethics and opportunities of AI.

## Lesson: Bias, deepfakes and misinformation (9 min)
AI is powerful — which means it can also cause harm if misused or poorly designed.

### Bias and fairness
AI can reproduce and amplify unfairness in its data. Examples that have been reported around the world:
- Hiring tools that favoured men because past hiring data did.
- Face-recognition systems with higher error rates for darker-skinned women.
- Language tools that work far better in English than in African languages, because they have less training data for them.

Fair AI needs **diverse data**, **testing across different groups** and **diverse teams** building it — including Africans building AI for African contexts.

### Deepfakes
**Deepfakes** are AI-generated or AI-altered videos, images or audio that make it look like someone said or did something they didn't. They have been used for scams (fake voice calls from "family members" asking for money), fake celebrity endorsements and political misinformation.

How to protect yourself:
- Be suspicious of shocking videos or audio, especially when they push you to act or share urgently.
- Look for oddities: unnatural blinking, mismatched lip movement, strange hands, blurry edges, robotic voice tone.
- **Verify** with trusted news sources or by contacting the person through a known channel.
- Remember the "family password" idea from our Cybersecurity course.

### Misinformation at scale
AI makes it easy to produce huge amounts of convincing fake text, reviews and news. Practise the **CRAAP test** from Digital Literacy Essentials: check the source, date, evidence and purpose before believing or sharing.

> [!WARNING]
> Creating deepfakes of real people without consent — especially sexual or humiliating content — is deeply harmful and can be illegal. Never create or share it.

## Lesson: AI ethics and responsible use (8 min)
**AI ethics** asks: how should AI be designed and used so that it benefits people and avoids harm?

### Key principles
- **Fairness** — AI should not discriminate unfairly against people.
- **Transparency** — people should know when they're interacting with AI and, where it matters, how decisions are made.
- **Privacy** — personal data must be protected and used with consent.
- **Accountability** — humans and organisations remain responsible for what their AI does.
- **Safety** — AI should be tested and used in ways that avoid harm.
- **Human oversight** — important decisions (medical, legal, hiring, loans) should involve humans who can review and override AI.

### Questions to ask before using AI for something important
1. Could this harm or disadvantage anyone?
2. Is the AI's output accurate enough for this purpose?
3. Am I sharing data I shouldn't?
4. Would the people affected agree this is a fair use?
5. Who is responsible if it goes wrong?

### Environmental impact
Training and running large AI models uses a lot of electricity and water for data centres. Use AI thoughtfully — for tasks where it truly helps.

### Governance
Countries and regions are creating rules for AI. The African Union adopted a Continental Artificial Intelligence Strategy in 2024, encouraging AI that supports Africa's development while protecting people's rights. Laws on data protection, like Ghana's Data Protection Act, already apply to many AI uses.

## Lesson: AI, jobs and your future (9 min)
AI will change many jobs — some tasks will be automated, others will be enhanced, and entirely new jobs will appear.

### What's likely to change
- **Routine tasks** — data entry, simple writing, basic customer queries — are increasingly automated.
- **Many jobs will be augmented**, not replaced: a teacher uses AI to prepare materials faster; a farmer uses AI apps to identify pests; a doctor uses AI to help read scans.
- **New jobs** are emerging: AI trainers and data labellers, prompt specialists, AI product managers, AI ethics and policy roles, and developers who build AI-powered apps.

> "AI won't replace you, but a person using AI well might." — a common saying in the tech industry

### Skills that stay valuable
- **Critical thinking** — questioning answers, including AI's.
- **Creativity** — original ideas and taste.
- **Communication and empathy** — understanding people and working in teams.
- **Problem-solving** — knowing which problems are worth solving.
- **Digital and data literacy** — everything you're learning on DBI Learn.
- **Learning how to learn** — the tools will keep changing.

### AI opportunities in Africa
African developers and entrepreneurs are building AI for local needs: tools for local languages, crop-disease detection for farmers, health diagnostics where doctors are scarce, and fintech fraud detection. Ghana has a growing community of AI researchers and start-ups. Your local knowledge is an advantage — you understand problems that people elsewhere don't see.

### Your next steps
1. Use AI tools regularly — and critically — in your learning.
2. Take our **Python** and **Data Analysis** courses; they're the foundations of most AI work.
3. Explore free courses such as **Elements of AI** (in the Resource library).
4. Start a small project: maybe an AI-assisted study guide for your class, or a chatbot that answers FAQs for a local business.
