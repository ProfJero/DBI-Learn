# Module: Digital marketing foundations
Understand the landscape, your audience and how to set goals.

## Lesson: What is digital marketing? (8 min)
**Marketing** is the process of understanding what people need and communicating how your product, service or cause meets that need. **Digital marketing** does this through digital channels: social media, search engines, websites, email, messaging apps and online ads.

### Traditional vs digital marketing
| Traditional | Digital |
|---|---|
| Radio, TV, newspapers, billboards, flyers | Social media, search, websites, WhatsApp, email |
| Often expensive | Can start for free; ads can start small |
| Hard to measure who saw it | Detailed data on views, clicks and sales |
| One-way message | Two-way conversation with customers |
| Broad audience | Precise targeting (location, age, interests) |

Both still matter — a radio ad plus a strong Facebook page often works better than either alone.

### The main digital channels
- **Social media** — Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, YouTube.
- **Messaging** — WhatsApp (hugely popular in Ghana), Telegram.
- **Search engines** — Google; people searching for what you offer *right now*.
- **Websites and online stores** — your own space online.
- **Email and SMS** — direct communication with customers who opted in.
- **Online marketplaces** — Jumia, Tonaton and others.

### Owned, earned and paid media
- **Owned** — channels you control: your page, website, WhatsApp catalogue, email list.
- **Earned** — free attention: shares, reviews, word of mouth, news stories.
- **Paid** — ads and sponsored posts.

Build owned channels first — they're yours, even if an algorithm changes.

## Lesson: Know your audience (9 min)
The biggest marketing mistake is trying to reach "everyone". When you speak to everyone, you connect with no one. Instead, define your **target audience**.

### Creating a customer persona
A **persona** is a short profile of your ideal customer. Example for a shop selling affordable school bags:

> **Name:** Auntie Efua
> **Age:** 38, mother of three
> **Location:** Cape Coast
> **Job:** Trader at the market
> **Goals:** Durable bags that last the whole school year, good value
> **Frustrations:** Bags that tear after two months; no time to travel to Accra
> **Online habits:** WhatsApp all day, Facebook in the evening, watches TikTok videos
> **Decides by:** Recommendations from other parents, seeing the product in videos

Now you know where to market (WhatsApp, Facebook, TikTok), what to say (durability, value, delivery), and when (evenings, before school terms start).

### Questions to ask about your audience
- Who are they? (age, location, occupation, language)
- What problems do they have that you solve?
- Where do they spend time online?
- What makes them trust a business?
- What stops them from buying? (price, trust, distance)

### Research methods
- Talk to 5–10 existing customers.
- Look at comments on competitors' pages.
- Run a simple poll on WhatsApp status or Instagram Stories.
- Check Facebook and Instagram Insights to see who follows you.

### Your value proposition
Finish this sentence: *"We help [audience] to [benefit] by [what makes you different]."*

Example: *"We help busy parents in Cape Coast get strong, affordable school bags delivered to their door in 24 hours."*

## Lesson: Goals and the marketing funnel (9 min)
### The marketing funnel
Customers go through stages before buying:

1. **Awareness** — they discover you exist. (A TikTok video, a shared post.)
2. **Interest** — they want to know more. (They follow your page, visit your profile.)
3. **Consideration** — they compare options. (They read reviews, check prices, message you.)
4. **Conversion** — they buy, register or donate.
5. **Loyalty & advocacy** — they come back and recommend you.

Different content works at each stage: fun, shareable videos for awareness; product details and testimonials for consideration; offers and easy ordering for conversion; thank-you messages and loyalty rewards for retention.

### SMART goals
Vague goals like "get more followers" don't help you plan. Make goals **SMART**:

- **S**pecific — what exactly?
- **M**easurable — how will you know?
- **A**chievable — realistic with your time and budget?
- **R**elevant — does it help the business?
- **T**ime-bound — by when?

❌ "Sell more bags."
✅ "Receive 50 WhatsApp orders for school bags between 1 and 31 August by posting daily product videos on TikTok and Facebook."

### Key metrics (KPIs)
- **Reach** — how many people saw your content
- **Engagement** — likes, comments, shares, saves
- **Click-through rate (CTR)** — % of people who clicked your link
- **Conversions** — orders, sign-ups, enquiries
- **Cost per result** — for paid ads, how much each result costs

Followers are nice, but **sales and enquiries** pay the bills.

# Module: Social media and content
Create content people want to watch, read and share.

## Lesson: Choosing the right platforms (8 min)
You don't need to be everywhere. It's better to be excellent on two platforms than weak on six.

### Platform guide
| Platform | Best for | Content that works |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** | Direct customer contact, orders, updates | Status updates, catalogues, broadcast lists |
| **Facebook** | Broad local audiences, community groups, events | Photos, videos, events, community posts |
| **Instagram** | Visual products: fashion, food, beauty, design | Reels, high-quality photos, Stories |
| **TikTok** | Reaching new, young audiences fast | Short, authentic, entertaining videos |
| **YouTube** | Tutorials, long-form content, search | How-to videos, reviews |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional services, B2B, careers | Expertise, case studies, company news |
| **X (Twitter)** | News, conversations, customer service | Short updates, trends, replies |

### How to choose
1. Where is your **target audience**? (Look back at your persona.)
2. What **content** can you realistically create? (Photos? Videos? Articles?)
3. How much **time** do you have? Video platforms need more effort but reach more people.

### Optimise your profile
- Clear **profile photo** (your logo) and **cover image**.
- A **bio** that explains what you do, where and how to order: *"Strong, stylish school bags 🎒 | Cape Coast | Delivery in 24h | WhatsApp 024 000 0000"*.
- **Link** to your WhatsApp, website or catalogue.
- Use the same name/handle across platforms so people can find you.

> [!TIP]
> On Facebook and Instagram, switch to a **professional/business account** for free access to Insights (analytics) and contact buttons.

## Lesson: Creating engaging content (10 min)
Great content either **educates**, **entertains**, **inspires** or **helps** — ideally more than one.

### Content ideas that work
- **Behind the scenes** — how your product is made, your team at work.
- **How-to / tips** — "3 ways to style one kaba", "How to make your school bag last all year".
- **Customer stories and testimonials** — with permission.
- **Before and after** — hair, renovations, designs.
- **Product showcases** — features, prices, colours (always show the price — people hate asking).
- **Questions and polls** — "Which colour should we restock: red or blue?"
- **Trends and challenges** — join relevant trending sounds or topics, with your own twist.
- **Offers** — limited-time discounts, bundles, free delivery days.

### The 80/20 rule
About **80%** of your content should give value (help, entertain, inform) and **20%** should directly sell. If every post says "Buy now!", people stop paying attention.

### Short video is king
Reels, TikToks and WhatsApp status videos get the most reach. Tips:
- **Hook in the first 2 seconds** — start with movement, a question or a surprising result.
- Film **vertically** (9:16).
- Keep it **15–45 seconds**.
- Use good **natural light** (face a window) and clear sound.
- Add **captions** — many people watch without sound.
- End with a **call to action**: "Send us a WhatsApp to order 👇".

### Writing good captions
- First line = hook.
- Short paragraphs and line breaks.
- Clear call to action.
- 3–5 relevant hashtags, mixing popular and local ones (#AccraFood, #MadeInGhana).
- Write in the language your audience uses — English, Twi, Pidgin or a mix.

### Plan with a content calendar
Plan a week or month ahead in a simple spreadsheet: date, platform, content idea, caption, status. **Consistency beats intensity** — three good posts a week, every week, beats ten posts one week and nothing the next.

## Lesson: Community management and engagement (8 min)
Social media is *social*. Brands that talk with their audience build loyal customers.

### Respond quickly
- Reply to comments and messages **within a few hours** — slow replies lose sales.
- Use WhatsApp Business **quick replies** and Facebook **saved replies** for common questions (price, location, delivery).
- Thank people who share or tag you.

### Handle complaints professionally
Every business gets complaints. Handle them well and they can become loyal customers.

1. **Respond publicly and politely** — others are watching how you behave.
2. **Apologise and acknowledge**: "We're sorry your order arrived late, Kojo."
3. **Move to private** to sort details: "We've sent you a WhatsApp message to fix this."
4. **Fix it** and follow up.
5. **Never argue** or delete reasonable criticism.

### Encourage user-generated content
Ask happy customers to post photos with your product and tag you. Reshare them (with permission). Real customers are more persuasive than any ad.

### Work with influencers
Influencers with smaller but loyal local audiences (**micro-influencers**, e.g. 5,000–50,000 followers) are often more affordable and more trusted than celebrities. Choose people whose audience matches your persona, agree clear terms, and make sure sponsored posts are labelled as ads.

### Grow a community
Facebook groups and WhatsApp communities can build loyal followings — for example, a "Cape Coast Parents' Tips" group sponsored by your school bag shop.

# Module: Tools and channels

## Lesson: WhatsApp Business and Google Business Profile (10 min)
### WhatsApp Business
**WhatsApp Business** is a free app designed for small businesses. Install it from your app store (you can use a separate number from your personal WhatsApp).

Key features:
- **Business profile** — address, opening hours, description, email, website.
- **Catalogue** — show products with photos, prices and descriptions. Customers can browse and send orders without leaving the chat.
- **Quick replies** — type `/price` to send a saved answer instantly.
- **Greeting message** — automatic welcome when someone messages you for the first time.
- **Away message** — automatic reply outside business hours.
- **Labels** — organise chats: *New customer, Order placed, Paid, Delivered*.
- **Status** — post daily products and offers that all your contacts can see.
- **Broadcast lists** — send a message to many customers at once (they only receive it if they saved your number).

> [!WARNING]
> Only message people who agreed to hear from you. Spamming strangers gets your number reported and banned.

### Google Business Profile
When people search "printing shop near me" or "salon in Ho", Google shows a map with local businesses. A free **Google Business Profile** puts you on that map.

1. Go to google.com/business and sign in.
2. Add your business name, category, location (or service area), phone and hours.
3. Verify your business (Google will guide you).
4. Add good photos of your shop, products and team.
5. Ask happy customers to leave **reviews** — and reply to every review.

Customers can then call you, get directions and see your reviews directly from Google Search and Maps.

## Lesson: SEO and websites (9 min)
**SEO (Search Engine Optimisation)** is the practice of making your website appear higher in Google's free (organic) results.

### How Google ranks pages (simplified)
Google wants to show the most **helpful, relevant and trustworthy** answer for each search. It looks at:
- **Relevance** — does the page match what the person searched?
- **Quality** — is the content useful, original and up to date?
- **Authority** — do other reputable sites link to it?
- **Experience** — does the page load fast and work well on phones?

### Keyword research
**Keywords** are the words people type into Google. Think like your customer:
- "affordable school bags Cape Coast"
- "how to start a poultry farm in Ghana"
- "web designer in Kumasi"

Use Google's autocomplete suggestions and the "People also ask" box for ideas. Longer, more specific phrases (**long-tail keywords**) are easier to rank for.

### On-page SEO basics
- Put the main keyword in the **page title** and **main heading (H1)**.
- Write a clear **meta description** (the summary shown in Google results).
- Create genuinely useful content that answers questions.
- Use descriptive **image alt text**.
- Make sure the site is **fast** and **mobile-friendly**.
- Link to your other relevant pages.

### Local SEO
For local businesses, the Google Business Profile, reviews and consistent name/address/phone across the web matter most.

## Lesson: Email marketing and paid ads (10 min)
### Email marketing
Social media platforms control who sees your posts. Your **email list** is yours. Email works especially well for B2B services, education programmes, NGOs and online stores.

- Build your list with a reason to subscribe: a free guide, discount or newsletter.
- Only email people who **opted in**, and always include an **unsubscribe** link.
- Free tools like **Mailchimp** or **MailerLite** have free plans for small lists.
- Write a clear subject line, personalise with the reader's name, and include one main call to action.

### Paid social ads
Facebook, Instagram and TikTok ads let you reach people beyond your followers with precise targeting.

**Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads basics:**
1. Choose an **objective**: Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, Sales — or **Messages** (great for WhatsApp orders).
2. Define your **audience**: location (e.g. within 10 km of Kumasi), age, interests.
3. Set a **budget** and **schedule** — you can start with a small daily budget.
4. Create the **ad**: a strong image or short video, a clear headline and call-to-action button.
5. **Launch, measure and adjust.**

**Boosting** an existing post is the simplest option; **Ads Manager** gives more control.

### Ad tips
- Test two versions (an **A/B test**) with different images or headlines and keep the winner.
- Video ads usually perform better than plain images.
- Target narrowly at first; expand when you find what works.
- Watch **cost per result** — not just likes.

### Google Ads
Google Ads show your business when people search specific keywords — ideal because the person is already looking for what you sell. You pay when someone clicks (**pay-per-click**).

> [!WARNING]
> Don't spend money on ads before your profile, content and order process are ready. Ads amplify what you have — good or bad.

# Module: Measuring and growing
Use data to improve, and build a marketing plan you can act on.

## Lesson: Analytics — measuring what matters (9 min)
Digital marketing's superpower is that you can **measure** results and improve.

### Where to find your data
- **Meta Business Suite / Instagram Insights** — reach, engagement, follower demographics, best posting times.
- **TikTok Analytics** — views, watch time, audience.
- **WhatsApp Business** — basic message statistics.
- **Google Business Profile** — searches, calls, direction requests.
- **Google Analytics** — website visitors and what they do.

### Key questions to ask every month
1. Which **posts** got the most reach and engagement? Why?
2. Which **days and times** perform best?
3. Where are **enquiries and sales** coming from?
4. Are we moving toward our **SMART goals**?
5. What should we **do more of** — and stop doing?

### Engagement rate
Likes alone can mislead. **Engagement rate** compares interactions to reach:

```
Engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ reach × 100
```

A post with 1,000 reach and 50 interactions has a 5% engagement rate — better than a post with 10,000 reach and 100 interactions (1%).

### Track sales sources
Ask every new customer: *"How did you hear about us?"* and record it in a simple spreadsheet. Use special codes ("Mention TIKTOK10 for 10% off") or separate WhatsApp links to see which channel brings orders.

> [!TIP]
> Make a monthly one-page report: top three posts, key numbers vs last month, and three actions for next month. This is exactly what employers and clients want from a digital marketer.

## Lesson: Ethics, trust and the law (8 min)
Trust is a business's most valuable asset. Honest marketing builds it; deceptive marketing destroys it — fast.

### Be honest
- Don't make false claims ("cures all diseases", "guaranteed 100% profit").
- Show real prices and fees — no hidden charges.
- Use real photos of your products, not misleading images from the internet.
- Don't buy fake followers or reviews — platforms detect them, and they don't buy anything.

### Respect privacy and consent
- Only add people to broadcast lists or email lists with permission.
- Protect customer data (phone numbers, addresses) — Ghana's Data Protection Act applies to businesses.
- Ask permission before posting photos of customers, and be especially careful with children.

### Disclose sponsorships
If an influencer is paid or gets free products, the post should clearly say so (e.g. #ad, "Paid partnership"). Hidden ads mislead audiences.

### Respect copyright
Use music, images and videos you have the rights to. Platforms provide licensed music libraries for business accounts.

### Be culturally aware
Avoid content that mocks groups of people, religions or tribes. Check that jokes and trends are appropriate for your brand and audience. A single offensive post can go viral for the wrong reasons.

## Lesson: Project — your digital marketing plan (12 min)
Bring everything together in a one-page **digital marketing plan** for a real or imaginary business.

### Template
**1. Business summary**
What you sell, where, and your value proposition.

**2. Target audience**
Your customer persona (age, location, needs, online habits).

**3. SMART goals**
Two or three goals with numbers and deadlines.

**4. Channels**
Your two or three main platforms and why — for example WhatsApp Business (orders), TikTok (awareness), Google Business Profile (local search).

**5. Content plan**
Content themes (e.g. tips, behind-the-scenes, testimonials, offers), posting frequency, and a sample week.

**6. Tools**
WhatsApp Business, Canva (design), CapCut (video editing), Meta Business Suite (scheduling).

**7. Budget**
Free activities and any paid ads (e.g. GH₵X per week for two weeks to test a Messages campaign).

**8. Measurement**
Which metrics you'll track weekly and monthly, and how you'll know you succeeded.

### Example goal and actions
*Goal:* 60 WhatsApp orders for school bags in August.
*Actions:* daily TikTok/Reels showing durability tests; WhatsApp status every morning; Facebook post in two parents' groups weekly (with admin permission); a two-week Messages ad targeting parents within 15 km; a "Back-to-school bundle" offer in the last week of August.
*Measure:* orders per week, cost per WhatsApp conversation, top-performing video.
